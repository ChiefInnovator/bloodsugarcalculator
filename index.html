About A1C

A1C shows your average blood sugar over the past 2–3 months.

A1C reference ranges:

  • Normal: <5.7%
  • Prediabetes: 5.7–6.4%
  • Diabetes: 6.5%+

About eAG

eAG converts your A1C into the same units used on your glucose meter.

Important Note

This tool is for educational use only. Follow guidance from your healthcare provider for all medical decisions.

A1C and average blood glucose conversion formulas

The American Diabetes Association conversion tool publishes the A1C-Derived Average Glucose (ADAG) equation used here:

  • A1C to eAG: eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A1C (%) − 46.7
  • eAG to A1C: estimated A1C (%) = (eAG in mg/dL + 46.7) ÷ 28.7
  • Glucose units: mmol/L = mg/dL ÷ 18; mg/dL = mmol/L × 18

Example: 28.7 × 7 − 46.7 = 154.2 mg/dL, displayed as 154 mg/dL and 8.6 mmol/L. Calculations keep full precision before rounding the display.

A1C and eAG calculator FAQ

How do I convert A1C to estimated average glucose?

Use eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A1C (%) − 46.7. Divide that result by 18 for eAG in mmol/L. For example, A1C of 7% gives about 154 mg/dL or 8.6 mmol/L.

How do I convert average blood glucose to A1C?

Use estimated A1C (%) = (average glucose in mg/dL + 46.7) ÷ 28.7. For mmol/L, multiply the average by 18 first. An average of 154 mg/dL gives an estimated A1C of about 7.0%.

What do A1C and eAG mean?

A1C, also called HbA1c, reflects average blood glucose over roughly three months. Estimated average glucose (eAG) expresses the A1C-based estimate in mg/dL or mmol/L, the units used by glucose meters.

What is the average blood sugar for an A1C of 7%?

An A1C of 7% corresponds to an estimated average glucose of about 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L). This average is not a target for every individual reading.

Can I calculate A1C from one blood sugar reading?

No. One fasting, after-meal, or random glucose reading is not a long-term average. Enter an average glucose value for the reverse estimate; this conversion does not replace a laboratory A1C test.

How do I convert mg/dL to mmol/L?

For glucose, divide mg/dL by 18 to estimate mmol/L; multiply mmol/L by 18 to estimate mg/dL. These are glucose units. This calculator accepts A1C as a percentage, not HbA1c in mmol/mol.

Why might my A1C estimate differ from my meter or CGM average?

The equation estimates a population relationship, not an exact personal match. Averages also hide highs and lows. Changes in red blood cells, some hemoglobin variants, and certain health conditions can affect A1C. Discuss mismatches with your healthcare professional.

Can this calculator diagnose diabetes or set my treatment target?

No. It converts values for education and does not diagnose diabetes, recommend medication doses, or choose a personal glucose target. Ask your healthcare professional to interpret your results.

What values does the calculator accept?

A1C input accepts 4–20%. Average glucose input accepts 70–600 mg/dL or 3.9–33.3 mmol/L. These software input limits are not healthy ranges or treatment targets.

Is the calculator free, and what happens when I share a result?

The calculator is free and runs calculations in your browser without an account. Sharing is optional. A shared link includes readable calculation values; anyone with the link can access them.
